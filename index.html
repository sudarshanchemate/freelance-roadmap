The roadmap

10 steps to your first client

Tap a step to open it. Each one ends with a single action. Tick it when done; your progress is saved on this phone.

  1. 1
    • Don't sell "everything". Sell one clear service, such as "websites for local businesses" or "Reels editing for creators".
    • Choose a skill you can already deliver at a basic level. You'll learn the rest on real projects.
    • Pick a niche (gyms, cafés, doctors, coaches, real estate). "Websites for clinics" is easier to sell than "websites for anyone".

    ✍️ Build your one-liner

    I help local gyms get more members by building a fast, mobile-friendly website.
  2. 2
    • Give yourself 2–4 weeks of focused learning, 1–2 hours a day.
    • Follow the 70/30 rule: 30% watching tutorials, 70% building things yourself.
    • Learn only what your service needs. A wedding-reel editor doesn't need colour grading for films on day one.
    • Learn the "business" side too: how to talk to clients, write a clear message, and say no politely.
    Stuck in "tutorial hell"? Pick one real business near you and redesign or remake their stuff as practice. That becomes your portfolio too.
  3. 3
    • Make 3 sample projects that look like real client work, for example a café website, a clinic website, or a gym landing page.
    • Or do 1–2 projects free or cheap for a friend's or relative's business in exchange for a testimonial.
    • For each project, write a 3-line story: the problem → what you made → the result. Clients love before/after.
    3 strong projects in your niche beat 15 random ones. Quality and relevance win.
  4. 4
    • A simple portfolio page with your service, projects, and a contact button.
    • An Instagram or LinkedIn profile that clearly states what you do. Use your one-liner as your bio.
    • A WhatsApp Business account with a catalogue, a greeting message and quick replies.
    • A professional email (yourname.work@gmail.com is fine to start).
    • Pin 3 posts: your best work, a client result, and "how to work with me".
  5. 5
    • Warm network first: tell family, friends, seniors and teachers what you do.
    • Local businesses: search Google Maps for businesses with no website or a weak one, then message them a short video showing what you'd improve.
    • Content: post your work and your learning process on Instagram and LinkedIn.
    • Platforms: Fiverr, Upwork, and Contra. Competition is heavy at the start, so treat them as a bonus channel.

    First message (copy & edit)

    Hi [Name], I came across [Business] on Google Maps. Your reviews are great! I noticed [your website is slow on mobile / you don't have a website yet], so customers searching online might be going to competitors. I made a quick 1-minute video showing how I'd fix it. Can I send it here? – [Your name], [your service]

    Follow-up after 3 days

    Hi [Name], just following up on my last message. I'd be happy to share that short video with ideas for [Business]. No pressure at all, just let me know if it's useful 🙂
    Personalise the first line every time. 5 thoughtful messages beat 50 copy-paste ones.
  6. 6

    Beginner ranges in India, as a rough guide:

    Simple business website₹5,000–15,000
    Social media post design₹300–1,000
    Reel edit₹500–2,000
    Blog article (1,000 words)₹500–2,000
    Social media management / month₹5,000–15,000
    • Prices vary by city, niche and quality.
    • Raise your price after every 2–3 happy clients.
    • Offer 3 packages (Basic, Standard, Premium). Most clients pick the middle one. See an example ↓
  7. 7
    • Do a 15-minute discovery call: what does the client need, by when, and with what budget?
    • Send a written quotation the same day.
    • Always take a 50% advance before starting.
    • Put everything in writing, even if it's only on WhatsApp.

    📄 Every quotation must include

    • Scope: exactly what you will deliver (and what you won't)
    • Timeline with a delivery date
    • Price and payment terms (50% now, 50% on delivery)
    • Number of revision rounds (e.g. 2)
    • What the client must provide (logo, photos, text)
  8. 8
    • Share progress updates every 2–3 days, even if it's just a screenshot.
    • Agree on a limit of 2 revision rounds. Anything new beyond that is charged extra.
    • Deliver on time, even if it means going a little early.
    • Keep the domain, hosting, and accounts in the client's name.
    • Send a handover: files, logins, a short "how to use it" video, and your support terms.
    Underpromise, overdeliver. Say 7 days, deliver in 6. That's how you get referrals.
  9. 9
    • Send a proper invoice and collect payment through UPI or bank transfer.
    • For foreign clients, use a service like Payoneer, Wise or PayPal, and keep the payment records your bank gives you.
    • Keep a separate bank account for freelance income.
    • Track income and expenses in a simple sheet.
    • GST registration becomes mandatory only above the turnover threshold (currently ₹20 lakh for services in most states), but talk to a CA once you're earning regularly.
    Freelance income is taxable. File your ITR every year, even if the amount is small. It builds a record that helps with loans and visas later.
  10. 10
    • Ask every happy client for a testimonial, a Google review, and 2 referrals.
    • Offer monthly maintenance or support for recurring income (retainers).
    • Turn each project into content: before/after shots, a walkthrough reel, a case study.
    • Create reusable templates to deliver faster, then raise your price.
    • Later: build a small team, sell digital products, or teach what you learnt.

    Testimonial request

    Hi [Name], it was great working with you on [project]! 🙏 Could you share 2–3 lines about your experience? It really helps a fresher like me. And if you know anyone who needs [service], I'd be grateful for an intro.

Avoid these

Mistakes that cost freshers

Most beginners lose clients (or money) to these.

  • ✕Working without an advanceNo advance, no start. 50% is standard.
  • ✕Allowing unlimited revisionsFix the number in your quote. Extra rounds are paid.
  • ✕Underpricing foreverCheap is fine for 2–3 clients, then raise prices.
  • ✕Selling 10 services at onceOne clear service is easier to sell and to master.
  • ✕Not following upMost deals close on the second or third message.
  • ✕Disappearing mid-projectEven a "running 1 day late, sorry" message saves trust.
  • ✕Buying courses before getting clientsFree resources are enough to land your first 3 clients.
FAQ

Questions freshers ask

Do I need a degree or certificate?

No. Clients care about what you can deliver. Three good sample projects and one testimonial matter more than any certificate.

Can I freelance while in college?

Yes. Start with 1–2 hours a day and small projects. Be honest with clients about your available time and set realistic deadlines.

Should I start on Fiverr/Upwork or with local clients?

Start local and with your network: less competition and more trust. Use platforms in parallel as a bonus channel once you have some portfolio pieces.

What if a client doesn't pay?

That's why you take 50% upfront and share final files only after full payment. Share watermarked previews or a staging link until then.

How do I handle a client who keeps asking for changes?

Point to the quotation politely: "We agreed on 2 revision rounds. Happy to do this one, it'll be ₹___ extra." Clear scope keeps the relationship friendly.

How long until I get my first client?

If you send 5 personalised messages a day, most freshers land one within 30–60 days. Consistency is the whole game.
